# External references and reuse permissions

A reference credits and identifies a source. Permission to copy, modify or redistribute it comes from its applicable license, another verified permission, or a relevant legal exception. A link does not replace permission. Public visibility on GitHub does not itself grant general reuse rights. [GitHub licensing guidance](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/licensing-a-repository)

## How to handle a new source

Record the work's title, author as actually credited, source URL, exact version or commit, license or permission evidence, modifications, and whether the work is included in the shipped artifact.

- **An explicit license is present:** follow its conditions and preserve its notices and any required source-code delivery.
- **A standard license is explicitly declared but its text file is missing:** verify the declaration in the exact upstream release. Preserve that evidence and supply the referenced standard terms, clearly distinguishing supplied text from the publisher's original files. Do not invent copyright holders or years. [npm's supported SPDX declaration format](https://docs.npmjs.com/cli/v11/configuring-npm/package-json/#license)
- **No grant or uncertain permission:** keep a factual source link and mark the rights unconfirmed. Do not copy, bundle, relicense or publish the work through this project merely because it is credited. Obtain permission or choose an appropriately licensed alternative.
- **Special terms apply:** record them separately. Attribution does not waive noncommercial restrictions, hosted-service conditions or copyleft source obligations.

Do not apply Apache-2.0 to an upstream work on the strength of a reference alone. These records support the project's release decision; they do not establish ownership of an unknown source.
