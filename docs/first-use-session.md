# Observe a first use of Northern Bay

**Session ready; no observed participant results recorded.** Give each person only their role from the [assignment card](first-use-tasks.md). This page is for the observer.

## Before the session

- Use a different first-time person for each role. Do not let the reader watch the author. Plan a round of five first-time people per role in the same experience group. Keep returning users and anyone who has seen the answers separate.
- Agree to private note-taking and let participants stop. Use a fresh browser session, the fictional case and a new [scorecard](first-use-scorecard.md) outside the repository. Use participant codes, no names. Do not record screens by default or upload raw notes, handovers or identifying quotations.
- Record the release, browser and whether it is hosted or offline. The [build manifest](https://flaritycat.github.io/Arctic-Console-Open-Source-Project/BUILD.json) gives the hosted source commit. Do not pool builds, roles, experience groups or different reader inputs.
- For a paired exercise, use the author's actual fictional handover. For a standardized reader exercise, save the [synthetic review file](https://flaritycat.github.io/Arctic-Console-Open-Source-Project/northern-bay-review.json) (also named `northern-bay-review.json` in the offline ZIP). This example contains a deliberately questionable conclusion. Give it to the reader without that explanation. If an author cannot finish, keep that outcome. A fallback reader trial does not count as a successful paired handover.

## During the task

Read the assignment without pointing to controls or explaining the sampling issue, checksum or file transfer. Start timing when the participant has the task and page. Stop when they declare completion, stop trying or reach the agreed time limit. Note the first assistance before helping. “What did you expect?” is neutral; naming a button or answering a task question is help.

After the participant commits to an answer, ask: **What does the evidence let you say? What would you check next? What has happened to the assignment so far?** For a reader, also ask **What did the file check tell you?** Record their explanation before discussing the exercise. A click on Finish does not establish understanding.

| Core task | Observable completion without a hint |
| --- | --- |
| A1. Inspect evidence | Opens both sources and notices that the locations differ between days. |
| A2. Form an assessment | Separates the sample means from a whole-bay claim, names a relevant limitation and proposes a useful check. Exact wording and decision can vary. |
| A3. Leave work for the next shift | Produces a handover rather than a draft, can locate the file and explains what still needs to happen for someone to receive it. |
| R1. Open and examine the handover | Independently opens it in a fresh reader and consults both sources. |
| R2. Judge the finding | Explains what the observations support; qualifies a whole-bay claim if present. Recognizes that the checksum does not validate the finding or identify the author. |
| R3. Explain the outcome | Gives a next check and distinguishes finishing the exercise from notifying the sender or accepting ownership. |

A whole-bay change asserted as established, a checksum treated as proof of authorship or scientific correctness, or a claim that the tool sent or transferred responsibility is a **critical error**. Retain it even if the participant recovers with help. Explain the limitations after scoring.

## Decide what changes

Tally each core task separately. Review after five first-time attempts per role, experience group and input condition: require at least four unaided completions out of five on **each** task and zero critical errors. Set the round size before observing; for larger rounds require at least 80% per task. Keep helped, incorrect and stopped attempts. Missing scores or inconsistent evidence preclude a pass.

The observer records what happened. The maintainer reviews the aggregate and decides whether to recruit, fix and retest, or proceed to the next evaluation. Tie each fix to a de-identified finding: triggering task, behavior, change and retest with fresh people. Keep the build and denominator with every round. Scripted checks do not count as human attempts; passing this exercise would not qualify a real data product or authorize live Operations.
